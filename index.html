TLDR
CPA is up 47% since January. Both creative ageing and audience demand softening are driving it.
Internal (controllable)
  • Dec's 54 winners fuelled Jan's £79 CPA. That pool is now ageing
  • Feb hit rate was actually the strongest month at 16.3% — but winner count still low (40) vs Dec (58) due to fewer launches
  • Offer creative is masking severity: without it, blended CPA would be £141 not £122 — saving ~£19/purchase
  • Biotic CPA has nearly doubled — £131 Oct → £214 Mar, now 17.9% of spend
External (market)
  • CPM has fallen 13% since Jan — inventory is actually cheaper
  • Conversion rate has fallen 41% — the audience is less responsive
  • Even brand new creative is debuting at £118+ CPA vs £72 in Jan
Fixing the winner pool will help — but CVR headwinds mean CPA is unlikely to fully recover to Jan levels without broader demand recovery.

Creative segment analysis

Weekly CPA & spend share · Oct 2025 – Mar 2026
Celeb / ambassador vs non-celeb CPA
Celeb CPA looks higher — but celeb ads scale further and drive more purchases
  • Celeb median CPA is £100 vs £115 for non-celeb — cheaper per ad once you control for scale
  • Celeb ads attract 2× more spend per ad (avg £8,686 vs £6,347) and drive 2× more purchases per ad (97 vs 81 lifetime)
  • In Jan, celeb averaged 33 purchases per ad vs 16 for non-celeb — the volume efficiency is where the value lies
  • Celeb spend share has fallen from 49% (Oct) to 33% (Mar) as the pool ages — half of all Jan celeb ads are no longer running
  • Risk: top 5 celeb ads account for 29% of all celeb spend in Mar — concentration risk if those ads fatigue further
